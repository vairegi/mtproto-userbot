BONUS ANSWERS (verified against deployed code)
----------------------------------------------
1) List sweeper re-fetching page 1 of popular: it REPLACES the whole page
   payload (cache.put is a full upsert) on every scheduled pass — not a diff,
   not a "only if new items" check. That's deliberate: nhentai has no
   changes-feed, and popular-today ordering genuinely churns. Cost is tiny
   (~7.6 KB/page); the expensive detail fetches are gated by the F3
   freshness check, so unchanged galleries on that page are never re-fetched.

2) Old items: they live on. Search pages are replaced wholesale, but every
   gallery:* detail row persists independently in Turso until its 30-day TTL
   expires (or never, for sentinel rows). Removing a gallery from a search
   page does NOT delete its gallery:<id> row — the Mini App can still open
   it via detail lookup, and Bot 2's scanner can still see it.

3) "Which page of which sort/tag found new items" — THIS ZIP. Every new
   gallery fetched now logs `🆕 NEW gid=<id> found on <sort> page <n>` in
   Render logs, plus a per-page summary `🆕 <sort> page <n> → N new
   galleries fetched`. Both feed a 24h ring in dash_counters.per_page_new,
   and every day at 10:00 IST the bot DMs each BOT1_ADMIN_USER_IDS a digest:

       📊 Daily discovery digest (last 24h)
       popular-today — 12 new
         · page 1: 9
         · page 3: 3
       tag:incest — 4 new
         · page 2: 4
       Total new galleries: 16

   Use it to see the real "discovery frontier" per sort — if date only ever
   finds new items on pages 1-2, you can cut LIST_MAX_PAGES further with
   confidence.

Drag the zip onto the repo, redeploy ONLY the ScraperBot (BOT 1) service.

  BOT1_DIGEST_TIME_IST=HH:MM — change send time (default 10:00 IST)
